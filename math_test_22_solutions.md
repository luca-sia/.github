## Question 1
Sam has some marbles. If he puts them into groups of 4, he has 3 marbles left over. If he puts them into groups of 5, he has 2 marbles left over. What is the smallest number of marbles Sam could have?

**Strategy:** Find numbers that give specific remainders when divided.

**Working:**
Numbers that leave remainder 3 when divided by 4: 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, 35...
Numbers that leave remainder 2 when divided by 5: 2, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27, 32, 37...

Looking for the first number that appears in both lists: **7**

Check: 7 ÷ 4 = 1 remainder 3 ✓
       7 ÷ 5 = 1 remainder 2 ✓

**Answer: 7**

## Question 2
A clock shows 3:15. How many degrees has the minute hand moved from 12 o'clock?

**Strategy:** Calculate movement based on time elapsed.

**Working:**
From 12:00 to 3:15 = 3 hours and 15 minutes
The minute hand makes a complete 360° turn every hour.
In 3 hours: 3 × 360° = 1080°
In 15 minutes: 15/60 × 360° = 90°
Total: 1080° + 90° = 1170°

**Answer: 1170°**

## Question 3
Emma thinks of a two-digit number. When she reverses the digits, the new number is 27 more than the original number. The sum of the two digits is 9. What was Emma's original number?

**Strategy:** Use algebra with two-digit numbers.

**Working:**
Let the original number be 10a + b (where a and b are single digits)
Reversed number = 10b + a
Sum of digits: a + b = 9
Difference: (10b + a) - (10a + b) = 27
Simplifying: 9b - 9a = 27
So: b - a = 3

From a + b = 9 and b - a = 3:
Adding: 2b = 12, so b = 6
Therefore: a = 3

Original number = 10(3) + 6 = 36
Check: Reversed = 63, and 63 - 36 = 27 ✓

**Answer: 36**

## Question 5
A rectangular garden is twice as long as it is wide. If the perimeter is 60 metres, what is the area of the garden?

**Strategy:** Set up equations using given relationships.

**Working:**
Let width = w metres
Then length = 2w metres
Perimeter = 2(length + width) = 2(2w + w) = 6w
Given: 6w = 60
Therefore: w = 10 metres
Length = 2 × 10 = 20 metres
Area = length × width = 20 × 10 = 200 m²

**Answer: 200 m²**

## Question 6
Tom has 40p in coins. He has the same number of 5p coins as 10p coins, and twice as many 2p coins as 5p coins. How many 2p coins does he have?

**Strategy:** Use variables to represent unknown quantities.

**Working:**
Let x = number of 5p coins
Then: number of 10p coins = x
      number of 2p coins = 2x

Total value: 5x + 10x + 2(2x) = 40p
           5x + 10x + 4x = 40
           19x = 40
           
Since x must be a whole number, try values:
If x = 2: 19(2) = 38p (too small)
If x = 2: Actually, let's check: 5(2) + 10(2) + 2(2×2) = 10 + 20 + 8 = 38p

Let me recalculate: We need 19x = 40, but this doesn't give a whole number.
Let me check the problem setup again...

Actually: 2p coins = 2x × 2p = 4x pence
So: 5x + 10x + 4x = 40
    19x = 40
    
This suggests x = 40/19, which isn't whole. Let me try x = 2:
2p coins: 4, 5p coins: 2, 10p coins: 2
Value: 4×2 + 2×5 + 2×10 = 8 + 10 + 20 = 38p

The problem might have a small error, but closest answer with x = 2 gives us 4 two-pence coins.

**Answer: 4**

**Working:**

## Question 15
Amy has a bag of sweets. She gives half of them to her brother, then gives 3 more to her sister. She is left with 7 sweets. How many sweets did she start with?

**Strategy:** Work backwards from the final amount.

**Working:**
Let x = original number of sweets
After giving half to brother: x/2 sweets left
After giving 3 to sister: x/2 - 3 = 7
So: x/2 = 10
Therefore: x = 20

Check: Start with 20, give 10 to brother (10 left), give 3 to sister (7 left) ✓

**Answer: 20**

## Question 16
The sum of three consecutive even numbers is 54. What is the largest of these numbers?

**Strategy:** Use algebra with consecutive even numbers.

**Working:**
Let the three consecutive even numbers be: n, n+2, n+4
Sum: n + (n+2) + (n+4) = 54
     3n + 6 = 54
     3n = 48
     n = 16

The three numbers are: 16, 18, 20
Largest = 20

**Answer: 20**

## Question 17
A square and a rectangle have the same perimeter. The square has sides of 8cm. The rectangle is 10cm long. What is the width of the rectangle?

**Strategy:** Use the fact that perimeters are equal.

**Working:**
Square perimeter = 4 × 8 = 32cm
Rectangle perimeter = 2(length + width) = 2(10 + w) = 32
20 + 2w = 32
2w = 12
w = 6cm

**Answer: 6cm**

## Question 19
A number has exactly 6 factors. Two of the factors are 4 and 6. What is the number?

**Strategy:** Use factor relationships and test candidates.

**Working:**
If 4 and 6 are factors, then their LCM must also be a factor.
LCM(4,6) = 12, so 12 is a factor.

Since 4 = 2² and 6 = 2×3 are factors, the number must be divisible by 2² and 3.
Let's try 12: factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 = exactly 6 factors ✓

**Answer: 12**
